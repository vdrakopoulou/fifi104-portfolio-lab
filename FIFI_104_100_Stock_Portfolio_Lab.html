Real inputs: 100 U.S.-listed equity securities, their issuer-reported sectors and the Price field from the iShares S&P 100 ETF holdings snapshot dated 24 September 2026, accessed 26 September 2026. Prices are frozen USD valuation inputs, not live quotes or guaranteed execution prices. Source company labels are shortened for readability.

Universe rule: retain the snapshot's equity rows, remove Alphabet Class C (GOOG) to avoid holding both Alphabet share classes, and exclude cash, money-market holdings, collateral and futures. This yields 100 stocks. The source ticker BRK B is shown as BRK-B for quote links. Sector terminology, including Communication, follows the issuer. This selected universe is not a recommendation and the classroom allocations do not reproduce the ETF's weights or returns.

Hypothetical inputs: your capital, allocated amounts, fractional-share availability and every price-change scenario in this 100-stock lab. The unequal example assigns the numbers 1 through 100 in a fixed shuffled order to alphabetically sorted tickers, then scales them to the budget. The mixed scenario uses a repeatable ticker-based classroom rule between -15% and +20%; it is not estimated from prices or financial fundamentals.

Calculation rules, rounding and limits
Fractional shares = allocated dollars / entry price
Whole shares = floor(allocated dollars / entry price)
Amount spent = fractional allocation, or whole shares x entry price
Cash = starting capital - total amount spent
Stock price return = scenario change / 100
Scenario ending stock value = amount spent x (1 + stock price return)
Portfolio ending value = cash + sum(ending stock values)
Portfolio P/L = ending value - starting capital
Portfolio return = portfolio P/L / starting capital
Contribution (percentage points) = amount spent / starting capital x scenario change

Dollar budgets are stored as integer cents. Automatic allocations use a largest-remainder method so the displayed budgets reconcile to the exact capital amount. Scenario values retain full precision internally; individual displayed rows can differ from a rounded total by cents. Cash earns zero and experiences no scenario shock. No borrowing, short selling, leverage, new flows, rebalancing, distributions, costs, taxes or FX are modeled. Scenario returns below -100% are blocked for these long-only holdings. The +1,000% input ceiling is a software classroom limit, not a financial limit.

Overallocated budgets are flagged and outcome calculations are blocked until corrected. In whole-share mode, a positive stock budget can buy zero shares; its full unused amount remains cash. No volatility, covariance, beta, Sharpe ratio or probability is inferred from these one-period scenarios. Today's research cannot validate an earlier investment choice without information-timing controls.
