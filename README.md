# FIFI104-portfolio-lab
# FIFI104 | Student quick-start

## Before you begin

Use your instructor's published **GitHub Pages** link, not the GitHub code page. A laptop or desktop is convenient for the wide stock table. You do not need to install software or create a GitHub account.

**Save before refreshing or closing.** Work is kept in the current browser tab, not automatically saved to your GitHub account or sent to the instructor. **Save notes to this lab** and **Capture design** do not replace **Save lab file**.

## 1. Open and set your capital

Open the published lab link supplied by your instructor. No GitHub account is needed. In Paper capital (USD), enter a practice budget such as 10000 and click Apply. Leave Scale existing allocations when capital changes checked to keep the same proportions.

## 2. Choose an allocation example

Keep the opening 100-stock example, or choose another option under Allocation example and click Load. Start with all cash lets you build from zero. Loading an example replaces your current stock amounts; save a JSON file first when you want to keep the previous design.

## 3. Investigate and allocate

Choose three companies to investigate. Click Research, record a dated fact and its source, then add one risk and your allocation rationale. Click Save notes to this lab. Edit Allocate USD amounts in the stock table. Stay within your capital; uninvested money remains cash. Fit amounts to budget rescales all nonzero allocations to use the full budget.

## 4. Calculate and challenge

Select a hypothetical scenario and click Reveal scenario result. Compare All stocks fall 20% with Largest actual stock position falls 30%; others flat. Keep the allocations unchanged for this comparison. Each scenario starts from the original allocation, not the previous result. Change to Whole shares only and a $1,000 budget to examine leftover cash and holdings.

## 5. Compare and defend

After revealing an outcome, give the design a name and click Capture design. You can keep up to three comparison cards. In Your investment pitch and conclusion, explain your weights, evidence, calculation, key risk and what would change your mind. Insert portfolio summary replaces the pitch text, so use it before drafting or after saving your existing work.

## 6. Save, resume and submit

Click Save lab file and keep the downloaded JSON file. To resume, reopen the lab, click Load lab file and select that JSON. Reveal the result before Export 100-stock CSV when you need scenario outcomes. Use Print / save as PDF for a readable report. Submit only through the channel specified by your instructor; the lab does not submit work automatically.

## Understand your files

- **HTML (`index.html`)**: the lab application; it is not your saved practice work.
- **JSON**: your resumable lab state, including amounts, notes, pitch, scenario and comparison cards.
- **CSV**: exported analysis for a spreadsheet; it cannot be loaded back as a lab save.
- **PDF**: a printable record; it cannot restore the interactive session.

The default save filename is `FIFI104_Portfolio_Lab_Save.json`. Rename copies after downloading, for example `Group03_Practice01.json`, keeping the `.json` extension. Only load trusted files from the same lab version.

## Practice, not trading

The supplied 100-stock lab labels its frozen entry-price snapshot **24 September 2026**. Scenarios are hypothetical; they are not live performance, forecasts or investment recommendations. Cash earns 0% in the exercise. The main lab calculations work offline when the HTML is downloaded; research websites require internet access. The separately embedded original five-stock exercise has different rules and dates.

## Privacy and submission

Do not include passwords, real account details or sensitive personal information. Keep saved work private and follow your instructor's submission instructions. The public repository is not an assignment drop box. The lab has no class gradebook or automatic cloud save. GitHub records visitors' IP addresses for security; external research sites have their own privacy practices.
